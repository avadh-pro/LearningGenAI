## 4. Distance-metric vs ANN-indexing clarification (verbal, not yet saved to a file)

**Confirmed correct:** the user's framing that Euclidean distance is the right choice specifically when *magnitude/size* is part of what you're measuring (e.g., customer spend clustering), versus cosine/dot product when only *direction/topic* matters regardless of scale — matches the existing decision rule already documented in `Introduction to Vector Database.md` Interview Q2/Q3.

**New clarification given (not yet documented anywhere):** distance metrics (cosine/dot product/Euclidean) and ANN indexing (HNSW/IVF) sit on two independent layers, not a single spectrum:
- **Layer 1 — the ruler:** the distance metric answers "what does *similar* mean?"
- **Layer 2 — the strategy:** ANN (via HNSW or IVF) answers "how do I find the nearest ones *fast*, without checking every vector?"

ANN is not an alternative to a distance metric — even brute-force search uses cosine/dot/Euclidean, just checked against every vector. HNSW and IVF only decide *which small subset* of vectors to check; the chosen metric still decides *how* those candidates get compared. Explained with a library analogy (IVF = pre-sorted genre bins you narrow into via `nprobe`; HNSW = a multi-level highway of shortcuts you descend through) plus a mermaid two-layer diagram.
